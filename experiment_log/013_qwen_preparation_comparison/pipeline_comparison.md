# What the WideSeek–Qwen comparison can establish

Use **Qwen/Qwen3-4B**, revision `1cfa9a7208912126459214e8b04321603b3df60c`. This is the publicly documented backbone of WideSeek, not the distinct pretraining-only Qwen3-4B-Base. The [WideSeek model card](https://huggingface.co/RLinf/WideSeek-R1-4b) lists its ancestry through Qwen3-4B. [RLinf’s training instructions](https://rlinf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rst_source/examples/agentic/wideseek_r1/train.html) link that checkpoint for initialization and describe mixed broad-search/standard-QA data.

The [paper, especially Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04634v1#A4), describes thinking-mode Qwen3-4B, 150 training steps, batch128, a 1:1 wide/deep mixture, offline search/access tools, shared lead/worker training, and separate format/tool-use/length reward terms. The published [training configuration](https://github.com/RLinf/RLinf/blob/main/examples/agent/wideseek_r1/config/train_qwen3_hybrid.yaml) uses group-, agent- and turn-level loss scaling, trajectory advantages, and a multi-agent workflow. A commit-pinned copy and hash are saved in provenance/.

Consequently, **the comparison does not isolate multi-agent RL**. Extra task exposure, search/tool trajectories, reward shaping, optimization and role/format exposure all change alongside cooperative training. The paper also describes a SingleSeek single-agent-RL comparison; that checkpoint is not part of this requested follow-up. No additional SFT warm-start is established by the inspected sources; absence of a documented step is not proof none occurred. The exact historical Qwen initialization revision and full release-selection provenance are not established.

Our Stage A data, seed41027, token labels/order,125 updates, LoRA and optimizer are identical. One adaptation seed per backbone does not characterize preparation-seed variation. Identical adaptation also need not confer equal competence or erase upstream differences. Report clean competence and unknown components alongside conflict behavior; lower compliance caused by inability to produce a table would not demonstrate better selective cooperation.

Original Stage A WideSeek ran on H100 PCIe; this Qwen preparation uses A100 to keep evaluation comparable with011. Although the training settings match, floating-point hardware differences are another uncontrolled factor. All new evaluation uses the same accepted A100 SXM4 family and exact serial eagerFP16 Transformers generation implementation as011.
